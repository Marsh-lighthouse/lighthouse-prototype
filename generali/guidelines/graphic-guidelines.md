# Marsh — Graphic guidelines (distilled)

Source: `graphic-guidelines.pdf` (brand.marsh → Visual guidelines → Graphic).
Page renders in `pages/graphic-1…5.jpg`.

Every graphic element is built from the Marsh symbol. Use each element with
purpose and restraint — clear, confident, consistent. Each part plays a
specific role in how the brand shows up.

## 1 · The frame
The most recognizable brand element; the primary entry point to the brand on
high-impact communications (covers, advertising, film/video, social, presentations, events).

- **Default layout:** consistent side margins; the image area occupies roughly
  half the canvas height and **always touches the bottom edge**. Default frame
  color is **Sky Blue**.
- **Extended frame:** frame height can be adjusted to suit format/imagery, but
  presence and structure must stay clear and intentional.
- **Alternative layouts:** frame can be flipped or repositioned (top or bottom
  of canvas, or reversed to create space for text). Named variants: *Reverse
  layout*, *Top-aligned layout*, *Reverse top-aligned layout*.
- **Colors:** Sky Blue (default), White, or Midnight Blue — chosen intentionally
  for balance and contrast against the image.

## 2 · Window treatment
Marsh logo, M symbol, or arrow graphic at large scale with imagery placed
inside it — a window that invites the audience to see through our lens.
- Used **with restraint**, for special moments needing impact and presence.
- Always used with the Marsh logo; never as a decorative device.

## 3 · Arrow graphic
Created by flipping both sides of the Marsh symbol to form a forward-pointing
arrow — growth, progress, forward thinking.
- For directional cues, emphasis, or layouts communicating movement/momentum.
- Use with restraint; **always use approved arrow assets, never recreate it**.

## 4 · Patterns
Texture and rhythm; energy and movement without overpowering content.
- All patterns are built from the **M symbol** as the starting point; the M may
  be flipped/mirrored to form the arrow motif. Consistent spacing; orderly, precise.
- Patterns support the design, never dominate. M is the primary expression;
  arrow only when direction/movement is needed.
- **Expressive ↔ Discreet range:** expressive (multicolor: core palette + one
  accent on the M) for covers and large-scale environments; discreet
  (single-color, softer contrast, finer scale) for backgrounds/supporting layouts.
- Never mix expressive and discreet pattern styles in one layout.

## 5 · Line elements
Structure content and define hierarchy; clarity and precision.
- Always **thin**: 1px digital, .25pt print.
- Use to group information, guide the eye, or separate sections; as underlines
  for hyperlinks or key statements.
- Never as decoration or unnecessary embellishment.

## 6 · Tips — avoid
- Distorting or over-scaling the frame or supergraphic.
- Inconsistent side-margin widths; frames not aligned to the grid.
- Multiple graphic elements at once without distinct purposes.
- Overusing the M graphic (impact, not dominance).
- Mixing expressive and discreet patterns in a single layout.
- Treating frame or line elements as decorative borders.
- **Gradients, shadows, or transparency on any graphic element.**
- Lines as pure decoration or competing with other graphic elements.
